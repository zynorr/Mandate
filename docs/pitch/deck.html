The problem 02

“It happened on-chain” is not the same as “it was authorized.”

A transaction hash proves something was submitted. It does not show who approved the target, the method, or the value — and it says nothing about whether that action was the one a human actually agreed to.

Agents move fast

Smart accounts and ERC-4337 UserOperations let an agent act without a person in the loop for every transaction.

Approval context is lost

The decision lives in a chat, a ticket, or someone's memory. The chain keeps only the execution.

Reviewers get a hash

Auditors are handed explorers and raw call data, then asked to reconstruct intent after the fact.

The gap is not another monitoring dashboard. It is a durable link between the approved action and the observed one.

The product 03

An evidence ledger, not a wallet.

Mandate records the action a human approved, the policy version that applied, and the public transaction or UserOperation that actually occurred. It then shows where the record matches, where it does not, and what evidence is missing.

  • Versioned mandates. Policies publish as immutable versions with content hashes.
  • Narrow intents. One target, one method, one native value, one approver.
  • Public evidence only. RPC, explorer, and bundler reads — nothing signed.
  • Exportable reports. JSON and Markdown with a report fingerprint.
Deliberate boundaries
  • No wallet connection, keys, or signing
  • No custody, gas sponsorship, or UserOperation submission
  • No on-chain enforcement — policies are review rules
  • No claim of success before a receipt exists
  • Testnet only; chain ID is validated against 968

Boundaries are a feature. A tool that cannot move funds is a tool a reviewer can trust with the record.

The core object 05

A comparison, field by field.

The unit of the product is a versioned mandate plus an evidence comparison — not a chat, an approval feed, or a transaction builder.

  • Intent match — did the observed call match what was approved?
  • Policy result — does it satisfy the pinned policy snapshot?
  • Chain result — did the receipt report success?
  • Attribution — is the sender the registered account?

Four separate signals. A successful transaction can still be out of policy; a failed one can still be attributed.

Review model 06

Six verdicts. The most conservative one wins.

Verdict What it means
matched Observed fields match the linked intent and the recorded policy snapshot.
out_of_policy The action belongs to the agent, but a target, method, value, or policy check differs.
failed The chain reports failure. The record is preserved without treating it as authorized.
unattributed No approved intent is attached. The action stays visible for review.
needs_review Coverage is incomplete. Missing evidence is never converted into a pass.
pending Not yet confirmed. No success claim is made before a receipt.

Status language is the product. “Submitted” and “succeeded” are separate states, and every observed field names its source with a fetched-at timestamp.

Coverage 07

What Mandate checks — and what it does not.

Checked
  • Chain ID is 968 and the observed sender matches the registered account
  • Target, method selector, native value, policy expiry, daily caps
  • Supported Kernel and SimpleAccount call decoding
  • Token movements, shown as context
  • Optional hash anchors for policies, intents, and reports
Not claimed
  • Unknown multicalls or opaque inner calls are marked partial coverage
  • Explorer data can be incomplete; gaps are labelled in place
  • Token amounts are informational, not evaluated by policy
  • Not an on-chain control — Mandate cannot block an action
  • Not custody, tracing, or a guarantee of authorship

Trust comes from showing what is missing, not only what passed.

Differentiation 09

Calm, documentary, and exportable.

Public research across agent-governance and execution-evidence products points to the same winning pattern: make the decision boundary visible — proposed action → policy → approval → execution → receipt.

What we adopted
  • Timeline first — ordered, timestamped events linked to policy and evidence
  • Split verdicts — intent, policy, chain, and attribution stay separate
  • Provenance on every field — source and fetched-at, always
  • Progressive disclosure — plain-language summary, hashes one panel away
What we refused
  • Wallet, key, and signing metaphors as the centre of the product
  • Dark terminal chrome and generic violet-gradient SaaS styling
  • Status language that conflates submitted with succeeded
  • Turning missing coverage into an implied pass

Visual direction: warm paper and editorial type, like an audit workpaper. Indigo marks authority, evergreen marks confirmed evidence, ochre marks review. The core object is a versioned mandate plus a comparison — never a transaction builder.

The registry stores a content hash, a kind, a recorder address, and a block timestamp. Policy, intent, report, and evidence hashes are written by a local burner key and read back by the app.

  • No owner, custody, enforcement, upgrade path, or deletion
  • First recorder of a hash wins; the app only ever reads
  • This deck's own SHA-256 is anchored as a report on chain

An anchor proves a hash was recorded by an address at a time. It does not prove authorship, correctness, or that the record is current — and Mandate says so in the product.
